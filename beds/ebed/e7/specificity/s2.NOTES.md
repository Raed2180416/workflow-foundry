# s2: blind E7 specificity solver #2 (event-sourced)

Written 2026-10-15 from the arm files alone (`/var/tmp/foundry-work/e7-blind/arm-files/`: TASK.md, SPEC-E.md,
CONTRACT.md, api/*.schema.json) and BED-CONTRACT v1.1 §3. I did not open anything else under `beds/ebed/e7/`, nor the
oracle, E8's solvers, the reference policies, or the other blind solver. No oracle or grader was run. Cost: $0.

## Design

- **One instance per incident.** Each instance keeps the incident's raw facts in its carry: frozen intake values,
  resolver updates, caller messages, joining alerts and human-task events, each with its event time and delivery
  order. It also keeps the approval tasks it created and the status of each effect key.
- **Replay on every tick.** `Replay` rebuilds the incident's history from the facts and derives every record the
  rules require: itsm changes, notifications, SLA records, breaches, escalations, approval tasks. Each record has the
  instant it describes and its window. An effect is emitted when its window has opened, unless its key is already
  committed or pending. Records whose window opens later are planned, and the earliest of them sets the single
  `wake-*` obligation. Deadline consequences (R6.3, R6.5, R7.2) are history the replay adds once `now` is past
  the deadline.
- **Business-calendar module** (part B): local wall-clock intervals per local day in an IANA zone through
  `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, with offsets cached per 15-minute bucket. An interval runs from the first instant the local
  clock reads its start to the first instant it reads its end, so a skipped hour counts 0 and a repeated hour counts
  its real length. The R4.4 example gives 60 and 180 minutes. Holidays close the whole day or `[from, to)` of it.
  Reaching a target stops at a closing time (S4).
- **SLA engine** (part E): R4.2 order at each change instant: changes, then cancel, stop, reset and pause/resume
  for each SLA attached earlier, then attach. Retroactive start and pause come from the incident's value segments.
  The planned breach is computed per S13. Breach and escalations come from the running intervals and are left open
  while the SLA runs.
- **Keys** are deterministic per logical effect, for example `INC-…:hold:<minute>`, `<slaId>:resume:<minute>`,
  `<slaId>:esc:<pct>`, `INC-…:n:<template>:<minute>`. Arguments are deterministic too, including notification text
  and the assign `ref`. A duplicate instance therefore converges on the same keys.

## Readings adopted where the spec left room (with citations)

1. **Transitions outside R8.2 are ignored** (R8.2, "The `itsm` system does not check transitions: that is your
   job", §2). This covers a hold that is not from `inProgress`, an accept that is not from `assigned`, and a resume
   that is not from `onHold`. A reassign to the group that already holds an `assigned` incident changes nothing, so
   it gets no record. This also absorbs repeated resolver updates (W1). An event id that has already been seen is
   dropped.
2. **A resolver `resume` ends any hold, including `awaitingCaller`.** R3.2 maps `resume` to `itsm.resume` without
   qualification. I read §3.4's "after a hold that was not waiting for the caller" as a description of the action,
   not as a filter.
3. **Same-instant order** (S5, S16): facts are applied in event-time order, then in delivery order. All changes at
   one instant are applied, then the SLA engine runs once. A deadline consequence is applied after the facts of its
   instant, and only on a tick strictly after the deadline (S1, S10). Its SLA records are dated at the deadline and
   use its window (R4.5 last paragraph).
4. **No caller, no caller notice.** R6.4 says this explicitly for `resolved`. Because `to` is "the caller's
   `email`" (§4.9), I apply the same rule to `awaiting-caller`, `closed-no-response` and `incident-cancelled` on
   alert-opened incidents. The state changes (R6.3 close, R6.6 cancel) still happen.
5. **Service-desk business time B** (R6) uses the `serviceDeskCalendar`'s own `tz`, or `policy.systemTimeZone` if
   the calendar is floating. R4.3's source list applies only to SLAs. If a floating SLA calendar finds no zone in
   its sources, the solver also falls back to the system zone.
6. **Escalation `to`** (R5.2): the incident's assignment group after the changes of the instant the notice becomes
   due, which is the threshold instant T, or the attach instant x when T < x (S12). `groupManager` is the group's
   `managerEmail`.
7. **`major-incident` `to`** (R7.2, §4.9) is the service's `owner`. If `owner` is a user id rather than an address,
   the solver uses that user's `email`.
8. **Thresholds** (R5.1): `floor(target × percent / 100)` business minutes. The threshold is reached at the first
   instant the running business time reaches it, inclusive at a stop, cancel or pause instant (S3).
   `elapsedMinutes` is the running wall time excluding pauses ("running … except while paused", R4.4).
9. **Reset** (R4.2 step 3, S15): the field change is a value comparison before and after the instant. A reassign to
   the same group is not a change. The "also attaches" sentence applies to definitions that have a `reset` whose
   field changed.
10. **Approval** (R7): `dueAt` of the first task is creation + `majorApprovalMinutes`, from the edition at creation.
    Re-asks use the same deadline. The solver re-asks once per counted task whose answer was unusable, withdrawn or
    rejected by the schema, as R7.2 permits ("may"). The answer time is the event time.
    `excludeUsers: [callerId]` is set only when the incident has a caller. The answer schema requires
    `decision ∈ {approve, reject}`.
11. **Alert join** (R1.2, S8) is judged on `itsm.incidents` at the round start. A candidate incident must be opened
    by an alert with the same `ci` and `check`, be in `new`, `assigned`, `inProgress` or `onHold`, have
    `openedAt ≤ t`, and have no resolvedAt, closedAt or cancelledAt at or before t. If several match, the latest
    opened wins.
12. **Unknown incident** (§3.3, S9): the optional `unknown-incident` notice is never sent.
13. **Retries** (§1 Platform):
    - `rate-limited` is reissued at the first whole minute at or after `result time + retryAfter`.
    - 5xx, `predecessor-failed`, and 400 `invalid-args`/`field-renamed` are reissued at once, with the same key and
      the field names the schema file shows now.
    - 409 and other 4xx are not retried.
    - `unknown`/`partial` lead to a `status()` read.
    - There are at most 6 attempts per key.
    - Before the first emission of any key, `status()` is read, so a key already committed (by a duplicate instance,
      or after a lost carry) is never sent again.
14. **Renamed arguments** (§1 API changes): canonical names are mapped to the names the current schema shows. The
    mapping uses rename metadata or a description if present, then elimination (one missing name and one new name),
    then position.
15. **Go-live** (§3.6):
    - The incident and SLA records are authoritative. Messages already received are replayed only "in shadow"
      (nothing is emitted) to recover history the records lack: the awaiting-caller hold instant, earlier values for
      a later retroactive pause, and whether the priority was ever 1.
    - The hold instant comes from those messages, else from an SLA's open pause, else from the `awaiting-caller`
      notice time, else from go-live.
    - A derived record whose window closed before go-live is not emitted. A derived record already present in the
      migrated records (breach, escalation, notice) counts as committed.
    - A migrated SLA's definition is taken from the edition in force at its `startAt`.
    - An approval starts for a migrated incident only if its priority first becomes 1 after go-live, or if it is
      priority 1, not major and was opened within 15 minutes of go-live.
16. **Instance life:** an incident instance closes once the incident is closed or cancelled and nothing is pending,
    planned or due. Messages for an incident with an open instance are merged into it, using `ctx.instances`; if
    several are open, the smallest instance number wins. Messages about a recorded incident with no open instance
    get `close` with its subject and recorded priority. Messages naming no incident get subject `null`.
17. **Duplicate instances:** when one message is delivered twice in one instant, both copies start instances. From
    its second tick, an instance that sees another open instance with the same subject and a smaller number ends
    itself. Their keyed effects were identical, so nothing was applied twice.

## Known limits (not resolvable from the arm's view)

- **Same-instant races:**
  - Two different alerts with the same `ci` and `check` in one instant both open incidents.
  - An alert at the instant its incident is resolved by an update, or closed by a deadline due at that minute, is
    merged and then cannot be re-routed.
  - A message about a migrated incident at the go-live instant finds no open instance and is lost.

  All of these follow from round-start reads (contract §3.6).
- **Unconfirmed formats:**
  - The output schema of `humanTasks` and `obligations` is not in the arm files. The solver uses
    `onTimeout: 'expire'`, `kind: 'wake'`, `anchor`/`dueAt` as ISO instants and `onDue: 'replay'`.
  - The `ctx.files` shape and the migration payload shape are read defensively: an object, Map, nested object or
    directory; `records{…}` or top-level tables, with `messages` or `events`.
- **Migration approvals:** human tasks from before go-live are invisible, so an approval that was already running
  at go-live is not continued (reading 15).

## Self-tests (work folder `/var/tmp/foundry-work/e7-blind/s2/`)

`node --test --test-concurrency=1 test/*.test.mjs` runs 20 tests, all passing:
- the calendar module, including the R4.4 DST example, holidays, S4 and zones that differ from +01:00;
- input shapes and renames;
- 11 end-to-end scenarios on a small host written from CONTRACT §3 (`test/minihost.mjs`, a fictional world in
  `test/world.mjs`):
  - intake, escalation and the caller deadline;
  - P1 alert, approval, a joining alert and a warning alert;
  - a duplicate report and a withdrawal;
  - rate-limit retry, a field rename and a confirmation;
  - a retroactive start with retroactive pause (S12, S13);
  - S7;
  - approval re-ask;
  - a moot approval;
  - a 400 rename in flight;
  - reset with breach at the stop instant (S2);
  - go-live continuation and a merge by number.

The expected values are worked out by hand from SPEC-E. They test my reading of SPEC-E, not the bed's oracle.
`test/perf.mjs` runs 60 messages, 44 incidents and about 580 ticks in about 0.5 s; the largest carry is about 2 KB.

The solver is built from `parts/*.mjs` by `build.sh`, which concatenates them into this one file.
