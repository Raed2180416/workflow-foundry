## Timeouts

**The limit was 300 s per call, and it was a default nobody set.**
- It comes from `src/models/port.mjs:112` and `src/models/providers/claude-cli.mjs:64`, both `timeoutMs = 300000`.
- Nothing in `run.mjs`, `models.mjs` or `b0-call.mjs` overrides it.

**It was not pre-registered.**
- PREREG §2 gives the single-call arms only "per-call CLI cap $1", while b0agent and fhand get 20 minutes.
- The kill rule (§6) only covers rate limits, so b0ncall losing 42% of its trials and b0call-haiku-med 31% triggered nothing.
- Timeouts were scored as failures, not retried. That matches PREREG §5 and the runner, but not `DESIGN.md` §5.2, which classes a model timeout as an environment fault.
- PREREG §7 estimated Haiku 5.5 single calls at under $0.01; the actual median was $0.029–0.044. The Haiku 5.5 single-call setup was never piloted at task size before freezing.

**How long the calls ran and how much they produced.**
- Timed-out calls left no partial data: usage is zero, because the CLI only reports at exit.
- Haiku 5.5 runs at a steady ~275 output tokens/s on both CLI versions, so each killed call had produced about 82K tokens. The largest call that finished was 83,666 tokens in 299 s.
- Finished calls (latency medians):

| arm | d3 latency | d3 output tokens | d4 latency | d4 output tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| haiku-none | 192 s | 52K | 251 s | 69K |
| haiku-med | 223 s | 62K | 279 s (max 300 s) | 76K |
| b0ncall | 249 s | 70K | 291 s | 81K |

The d4 figures only include calls that beat 300 s, so they understate the real d4 times.

**The expected answer is not large.**

| | export rows | true records | bed output budget | median answer text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| d3 | 85 | 37–45 | 5,500 tokens | 7.2K chars |
| d4 | 115 | 47–61 | 7,300 tokens | 9.8K chars |

- Haiku 4.5 emits about 60 tokens per row, which is just the answer.
- Haiku 5.5 emits about 615 tokens per row, so roughly 90% of its output is not in the returned answer. That holds even for the "thinking off" arm, so that label is doubtful at this task size. The journal does not show what those tokens are.

**Would the calls have finished? Very likely yes.**
- Scaling each arm's d3 times by 115/85 roughly reproduces the observed d4 timeouts: 2/18 predicted vs 4/18 for none, 9/18 vs 11/18 for med, 14/17 vs 14/18 for b0ncall.
- The longest scaled time is 312, 355 and 367 s. A 420 s cap would give zero predicted timeouts.
- Projected output (at most ~100K tokens) stays under Haiku 5.5's 128K limit. No finished call stopped on a token limit.

**This handicaps the baseline because of a harness setting; one-call answering is not inherently too slow.**
- Haiku 4.5 single calls take 34–44 s and Sonnet 89–127 s. The limit only bites Haiku 5.5, because of its 10× extra generation.
- So the success gaps between fhand-haiku and the three Haiku 5.5 single-call arms are inflated at d4. Success among finished calls (an upper bound, not an imputation) is 0.92 for med, 0.78 for none and 0.81 for b0ncall, against the reported 0.64, 0.69 and 0.47.

**The cost-per-success figures for these arms are also understated.**
- Timed-out trials are recorded at $0 in `results.jsonl` (b0ncall shows only its notes call) and not flagged as unknown cost. The journal does carry the "unknown cost, bound $1" marker, but `experiments/lib/arms/b0-call.mjs:17` drops it.
- PREREG §3 requires charging these at the bound, and b0-agent already does.
- With the bound, cost per success becomes $0.20 (none), $0.52 (med) and $0.94 (b0ncall), against the reported $0.044, $0.040 and $0.053. At an estimated real spend of about $0.045 per killed call, it is $0.051, $0.062 and $0.093.

## 6. What the result does and does not mean

### 6.1 In plain language
**What it means**
- **Cheap workflow against a strong single call.** On one synthetic task family, the Haiku 5.5 model ran a workflow that an Opus agent wrote by hand. It got 35 of 36 attempts right, at about 1.6 cents per correct answer. One call to the stronger Sonnet 5.5 model got 28 of 36 right, at about 32 cents per correct answer. That meets the pre-registered rule: at least as good, and much cheaper. [M]
- **The same workflow on Sonnet** got 36 of 36, at 0.67× the cost per correct answer of a single Sonnet call. [M]
- **One Haiku 5.5 call without a workflow** does about as well on the d3 tasks (16 of 18 at effort medium) but costs about 2.5× more per correct answer, because each call writes about 60K tokens of output, almost all of which is not in the answer. [M; I that this output is hidden reasoning]
- **On the d4 tasks, most single Haiku 5.5 calls were cut off** by a 300 s limit nobody chose. The large d4 gap says more about that limit than about workflows. [M/I]
- **Haiku 4.5 fails every task as a single call; Haiku 5.5 passes most.** [M]

**What it does not mean**
- **It does not show that a model can *build* such a workflow.** This one was hand-written by an agent who had studied the family on development seeds, so it is a ceiling.
  - Construction is measured by C0.
  - C0's dev round hints at a drop when Haiku builds: 11/16 against 16/16 for Opus, with K_c = 1 and no claim made. [D `research/construction-20261006/REPORT-dev.md`]
- **It does not show transfer.** It covers one family, generated by a bed Claude wrote, at d3–d4 only.
- **It does not show a workflow is *more successful* than a single call on the same model.** At d3 that gap is within noise, and P1 cannot resolve gaps below about 35 points.
- **It does not establish C1** (§6.3).
- **The workflow's own "pass" cannot be trusted as a success signal.** It said pass on its only failure.
- **It says nothing about a workflow rescuing Haiku 4.5.** P1 had no workflow-on-Haiku-4.5 arm.

### 6.2 Haiku 4.5 (0/36) against Haiku 5.5
- **The pre-registered comparison:** b0call-haiku-none against b0call-haiku45-none gives **+0.694 [0.472, 0.889]**, McNemar p = 0.004 (9/0). [M]
  - Haiku 4.5 costs *more* per trial ($0.055 against $0.030) despite a 10× higher list price, because Haiku 5.5 emits about 10× more output tokens. [M]
- **Caveats:**
  1. 22 of Haiku 4.5's 36 zeros come from the format rule, but removing the duplicate rows still leaves 0/22. On this task the gap is real, driven by wrong amounts and identity errors. [D]
  2. The two arms do not reason equally. Haiku 4.5 with thinking off writes only the answer, while Haiku 5.5 "none" probably reasons anyway. [I]
  3. Haiku 4.5 with thinking on was not run.
- **The PREREG "rescue" question is not answered by P1.** The development log (`/var/tmp/foundry-work/records-v3/dev-log.jsonl`) records the hand workflow on Haiku 4.5. [M from the log]
  - Pre-review version `a8`, dev seeds, 2026-10-07: 20/20 dev tasks at $0.10–0.13 per task.
  - Sonnet escalation occurred in 10 of those 20 tasks.

  This is development evidence only: the seeds were reused while developing, and the workflow version differs from the frozen one.
- **Consequence: the cheap model has moved.** [I]
  - Haiku 5.5 single calls already pass 78–89% of the d3 tasks, so on families this hard a workflow has little room to add success for Haiku 5.5.
  - Haiku 5.5 is not a stand-in for a weak (about 9B) model.
  - Testing "rescue" needs a weak-executor arm: Haiku 4.5, or an open-weight model once it is un-parked.

### 6.3 Implications for C1
- **What C1 says.** "Frontier-authored workflows executed by assigned open-weight models reach non-inferior verified success at much lower cost than the frontier executing alone." [D ROADMAP §4.1]
- **P1 matches only the "frontier-authored" part:** the workflow was written by an Opus agent. [D] It differs on everything else [I]:
  - the executor is Haiku 5.5, a hosted model, not an open-weight one (class S was parked);
  - the comparator is a single Sonnet call (class L), not the frontier executing alone (Opus);
  - no non-inferiority margin was pre-registered;
  - the experiment is under-powered.

  **P1 is therefore a directional pre-test, not a test of C1.**
- **Direction.** P1 is consistent with C1's cost side under every cost treatment. No C1 falsifier would trigger. The falsifier is an F/B0 CPS upper bound of 0.85 or more; in P1 the largest upper bound is 0.450 for fhand-haiku against the same-model single call (0.489 at d3), and 0.063 against Sonnet. [M numbers; I relevance]
- **Inputs for the Phase 3 pre-registration** [I]:
  1. Set an **explicit, equal wall-clock limit for every arm**, and pilot every arm configuration at task size before freezing.
  2. Pass `costUnknown` and `costUpperUsd` through every arm. Make the analyzer print every pre-registered stratum, and check the printed tables against the pre-registration before the run.
  3. Run all-frontier (Opus) and all-cheapest baselines, as ROADMAP §2.3 step 5 requires.
  4. Use an executor weak enough to need the workflow.
  5. Record thinking tokens. `usageOf` in `claude-cli.mjs` drops them. Probe on dev seeds what Haiku 5.5's extra output is.
     - Part of the cost advantage comes from the route: long hidden output on large single calls.
     - Cost ratios are therefore specific to the model and the route.
  6. Use an independent oracle, not run verdicts, as the success signal. Fix the status-check gap only in a *new* workflow version, on dev seeds.
  7. Use fresh test seeds (§8).

### 6.4 Implications for the router
- **On this family, a ready workflow beats every other mode per run** on cost per success [M]:

- **P1 is rung 0 evidence:** a development bed that Claude wrote, and a workflow an Opus agent wrote after studying dev seeds. [D rung table, `00-SYNTHESIS.md`]
