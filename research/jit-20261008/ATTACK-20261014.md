## 1. Ideological attack

### 1.1 The vision, restated in plain English

**What it is.**
- Foundry lives inside a harness the owner already uses: Claude Code, ARC or AMP.
- A chat stays a chat and an agent stays an agent. Our reading [I] is that most of the time Foundry does nothing.

**What it builds, and when.** Some tasks have structure a skilled person would hand-build:
- a pipeline;
- a judged branch;
- a fan-out and fan-in;
- a nested sub-flow;
- a node that owns an agent;
- a swarm;
- a wait for an approval;
- a game loop.

For those tasks a *cheap* model builds the bounded, typed workflow on the spot. That model is Haiku 5.5 today and an
open-weight model later.

**What it builds from.**
- the trace of an agent that already did the work;
- a library of patterns that earned their place with evidence.

Frontier models only demonstrate and adjudicate, and only rarely. "They never run the loop" is our reading [I].

**How it behaves once built.**
- **It verifies itself.** It checks itself with executable contracts and with evidence that does not come from the model
  that produced the answer.
- **It defers when unsure.** It hands the task back to an agent.
- **It heals.** When the world drifts or a latent defect turns up, it repairs itself and goes back over past outputs the
  defect may have touched.
- **It settles.** Over a stream of recurring tasks it reaches a durable state at least as good as the hand-curated
  workflow, at a fraction of the cost.

**Reuse.** Proven workflows are stored with their evidence. Other programs reuse them only where the workflow's input
profile says it applies. Wrong reuse is rare, and it is counted.

**Product surface.**
- a router with a cost/reliability slider that chooses between interpreting, compiling and reusing;
- swarms, sub-agents and a board;
- a UI;
- deployment through MCP.

**Proof standard.** Every claim is a measured capability number against a simple baseline. It is preregistered, held
out, replicated at K ≥ 2 and reported with its negative results.

**Where the repo's own designs depart from this restatement.** Seven points, so alignment is explicit rather than
assumed:

1. **Who pays for demonstrations.** Inside a host, "the interpreter is the host agent"
   (`design/HARNESS-INTEGRATION.md:19-22`) [doc]. Demonstration cost is then booked to the user, not to Foundry.
2. **"Frontier rarely".** This is met today by a mid-tier model on every warmup. The best records arm used a Sonnet
   interpreter (22/24); the Opus-interpreter lineage scored 18/24. The gap between them is McNemar 5/1, p ≈ 0.22, so
   "the interpreter tier decided it" is not established (D25) [data].
3. **What `flow.wide` fans out.** Its judge fan-out is read as model calls inside one run, not as a host Workflow
   fan-out. The owner has not confirmed that reading (`design/W-BED.md` O8; `HARNESS-INTEGRATION.md:48-50`) [doc].
4. **The board.** It is refused by design: "a shared mutable board between agents (rewritten as P19 `wide` with a typed
   reduce)" (`design/library/LIBRARY.md:492`) [code].
5. **Waits, approvals and cross-run state.**
   - Waits and approvals are parked (W-BED §3.7). FRONTIER-GAPS H2 prefers not to build `flow.await` [doc].
   - The engine nodes `state`, `wait.signal` and `session` are "listed, not scheduled" (`design/library/BUILD-ORDER.md:78-87`)
     [code].
   - They are nonetheless the top three corpus blockers, unblocking 144, 108 and 104 items.
6. **Open-weight models.** The policy lists three local models (`qwen3.5-2b-q4km`, `granite-4.2-3b-q4km`,
   `qwen3.5-4b-q4km`) [code], but no PROOF-LEDGER row names one as constructor or executor. The only open-weight
   evidence is Kev-0.8B as a judge, at or below the rule baseline on 4 of 5 ports [doc].
7. **Latency and wrong answers.** A10 says "latency, not dollars, binds" (PROOF-LEDGER A10), yet no row carries a latency
   budget. W, the cost of a wrong delivery, is undeclared (`FRONTIER-GAPS.md:424`, G4) [doc].

### 1.3 Pillar by pillar

**P1. Cheap construction.**
- **Hidden assumption.** Haiku's deficit lies in authoring structure, and a closed vocabulary plus a pattern library
  removes it.
- **Evidence says the opposite.**
  - Haiku authoring a script from a description scored 16/16 in C0 (B1-haiku), while the JSON IR scored 11/16
    (`research/construction-20261006/REPORT-dev.md:23-26`) [code].
  - The failures that cost us are leaf semantics:
    - identity-merge thresholds (D1, F01);
    - a date order read once for the whole task (F02, D20);
    - signs (F03);
    - exclusion sets (D23, D25).
  - The L6-a audit found that the pattern checklists name 6 of 21 recorded failure causes (PROOF-LEDGER D9 **N**).
  - In kinetics the Haiku builder produced 1 accepted version in 9 attempts, for $23.79. Every compile scored exact 0/N
    against its traces (D25 §3).
- **Strongest argument that it is wrong.** The library supplies structure, which Haiku already had, and does nothing for
  the leaves, where Haiku fails. Library-first can reach parity on a problem that is already solved and leave the
  silent-miss rate untouched.
- **What the composer literature says.** The cheap model's best role is proposal and ranking inside a search that code
  runs and constraints prune [sweep A 2, 3, 4; P]:
  - SyPet solved 30/30 benchmarks, against 13 for CodeHint;
  - LLM ∪ A*-pCFG reached 80.1%, against 49.8% for the LLM alone;
  - Auto-Pipeline's RL policy reached 76.9%, against 76.6% for search.

  None of those results says the leaves get better.
- **"Cheap" is false at every observed horizon.** Compile is 54% of S→H spend on records and 67–88% on kinetics. With
  interpreter-tier shadows, no JIT arm breaks even against the Haiku agent at the configured shadow floor (P6).

**P2. Trace-JIT.**
- **Hidden assumption.** A trace of an agent solving the task contains the procedure, and three traces are enough
  (`DEFAULT_JIT.warmup: 3`, `maxTraces: 8`, `experiments/jit/stream.mjs:35`) [code].
- **Evidence.**
  - D2: a weak interpreter cannot check its own compiled code.
  - Interpreter deliveries were themselves wrong, 2 of 7 in records H/H (D13).
  - There were 0 imports across 21 solvers, and helpers were re-derived 10 of 10 times (D13).
  - `shapeKey` gives the same key to d3 and d4, so the drift that matters is invisible to the reuse key.
  - Every kinetics compile scored exact 0/N against its traces, so acceptance rested on "clean + named" (D25 §3).
- **Strongest argument that it is wrong.**
  - A trace is a *sample of behaviour*, not a specification. Compiling from three samples is program induction from
    three examples with a noisy teacher.
  - The JIT metaphor assumes the interpreter is correct by definition. Here the interpreter is the thing being measured
    (Haiku agent 15/24).
  - The field's measured answer to induction from few examples is types, constraints and distinguishing inputs, not
    more samples. Brahma's distinguishing inputs run 1.5–12× faster than random inputs; I checked this in the
    downloaded text [sweep A 5; P]. Sweep A [P] reports that LLMs on SyGuS "overfit to the examples".
- **Where the pillar may hold.** Either with a stronger interpreter, which contradicts "frontier rarely", or inside a
  host whose user has already paid for the interpreter. That second option is an accounting convention, not a cost win.

**P3. Library reuse by input profile.**
- **Hidden assumption.** Input shape predicts applicability.
- **Evidence.**
  - The skills result: 0/21, all false successes (`docs/RESEARCH.md:98`).
  - Slice 1 falsely refused 5/18 (d3) and 13/24 (d4) in-profile packs.
  - Slice 2a's `TOLERANT_CHECK` now falsely refuses 0/18 and 0/24 (parents 341–346), and 0/72 and 0/96 (parents
    301–324). It refuses 24/24 and 96/96 plants, and 42/42 re-dated VERITAS packs. The options were tuned on these dev
    seeds (`design/FLOW-V3-SLICE2.md` §2.2) [doc, M by its author].
  - The profile still sees shape, not meaning: "a day/month swap inside `##/##/####`, a currency that changes what an
    amount means" passes (FLOW-V3-SLICE2.md, "Limits").
  - A precedent keyed on split signature harms about 0.5 tasks per ruling (D19).
- **The kill rule fires on zero wrong reuses.**
  - With the repo's own `wilson` (`experiments/lib/stats.mjs:18-24`), the 95% upper bound for 0/60 is 0.0602, above
    0.05.
  - The minimum n to pass is 73 with 0 wrong, 110 with 1 wrong and 142 with 2 wrong [M].
- **Strongest argument that it is wrong.** Applicability is semantic ("does the same rule apply?"), and the gate is
  syntactic. Case-based reasoning measured exactly this [sweep B 7; P]: similarity retrieval picked the most adaptable
  case 70% of the time with 45 cases and 12% with 120, while adaptation-guided retrieval reached 100%. Slice 2a fixed the
  false refusals, but not the semantic blind spot. The callee cannot yet certify its own applicability.

**P4. Self-verification without an oracle.**
- **Hidden assumption.** Silent wrong answers leave cheap fingerprints that need no oracle.
- **Evidence.**
  - No runtime guard, invariant or judge check deopted on any of ≥ 21 wrong compiled outputs (`FRONTIER-GAPS.md:248`;
    row 4 "Contradicted", `:873`).
  - Shadows ran on 4 of the 21.
  - The Haiku adjudicator returned no majority, and the wrong answer shipped (D2).
  - 3 of 4 H→H adjudications were unresolved.
  - Jev's errors correlate with Haiku's: φ 0.612 [0.505, 0.713]; P(Haiku wrong | Jev wrong) is 0.525 against 0.136
    under independence; and when all are wrong, 36 of 40 choose the same option
    (`research/judge-20261009/independence/README.md:11-23`).
  - In S→H s212 the Sonnet shadow and adjudicator agree to within 3e-5 on the same wrong answer (D25 §3).
  - The comparator is non-transitive (D16).
  - Adoption does not require acceptance by default (`stream.mjs:72`, `:1004-1015`) [code].
- **What works.**
  - The kinetics exclusion-set exactness: 13/13 wrong answers differ in the set (D23), and 2/2 in D25.
  - The R5 hash-total trailer: 0 misses in 1,500 wrong decodes.
  - The offline size profile: 6 of the static solver's 7 silent misses, because size moves with drift on this bed. With
    the size checks off, recall is 0 (`HANDOFF.md:120-124`).
  - The AST guard diff: 10 of 12 mutants caught, 0 of 10 refactors flagged.
- **Strongest argument that it is wrong.**
  - The oracle-free signals that work check an exact structure. Either the task carries it (a hash total, an exclusion
    set) or the code and data carry it (sizes, the AST).
  - Where the error keeps every shape (a merge threshold, a convention read), nothing fires.
  - The draft's "only task-carried witnesses work" overreached on this point; the critique's correction is kept.
- **"Independent evidence" has no independent source.** Beds, oracles, references, prompts and judges are written by
  Claude (`W-BED.md` O10). The only judge that is not Claude correlates with Claude.
- **Net.** The pillar is true for tasks that certify themselves and false elsewhere. The programme has no row that says
  which tasks are which; PROOF-LEDGER K3 adds one.

**P5. Self-heal and the retroactive sweep.**
- **Hidden assumptions.**
  - Faults can be classified before repair.
  - A heal does not make things worse.
  - Sweeps are cheap because replay is $0.
- **Evidence.**
  - Live auto-heal produced no promoted fix.
  - A heal removed the ability to refuse, which turned deopts into silent failures (D9).
  - Deopts do heal (D3, positive).
  - A shadow pair's regression label can itself be a wrong answer: s212 "would carry a wrong expected answer" (D25).
  - The designed sweep marks as suspect every ancestor "whose output consumed a suspect child". It then relabels every
    admission `wrong` (`LIBRARY.md:398-417`).
- **Problem with the designed sweep.** It over-marks callers whose output would not change, and it then feeds those
  labels to the applicability calibration [sweep B 4; I agree].
- **Strongest argument that it is wrong.**
  - Heal is a search over patches with a proxy objective (replay agreement), and proxies can be anti-correlated with
    quality. In Schwaller et al., the retrosynthesis model with the highest round-trip accuracy, 81.2%, received the
    worst expert rating [sweep A 8; P].
  - D9 is the local instance.
  - Without witnesses, the heal loop optimises a proxy. The sweep as designed would then corrupt the labels the reuse
    gate learns from.
- **New since the draft.** R3′ (`beds/wbed/r3p/impact.mjs`) gives exact sets of callers that inherit a defect and
  callers that do not. That is the substrate the sweep's early-cutoff question needs (§3 M5).

**P6. Durability at or above the human version, at a fraction of the cost.**
- **Hidden assumptions.**
  - Streams are long enough to amortise.
  - Durable states exist and are knowable early.
  - The comparator is the frontier agent.
- **Evidence.**
  - Only records S→H and O→H settle in the oracle view.
  - O→H's durability becomes knowable only at idx 23, after the whole stream's cost.
  - S→H is confirmed by idx 8 and then relapses.

## 3. Creative mechanisms

Each mechanism lists the blocker, the mechanism, its isomorph with source, the build, the experiment and decision rule,
the cost, and what kills it. M1–M3 are first; M4–M5 are next; M6–M7 are demoted to diagnostics; M8–M11 wait for their
prerequisites. The draft's M2 (a two-class applicability filter) is dropped: slice 2a's measurements supersede it (§8).

### M1. Output-invariance contracts and witness tags (P4; PROOF-LEDGER K3, K4)

- **Blocker.** Silent in-profile misses. No runtime check fired on ≥ 21 wrong outputs, and the profile sees shape, not
  meaning.
- **Mechanism.**
  - (a) Each family declares metamorphic relations, and the stream runs a variant beside the original on a sampled share
    of tasks. Disagreement is a deopt with kind `invariance`.
    - For records: shuffle row order (the output must be invariant); duplicate one record (the entity set must be
      invariant); drop the records of one singleton entity (the output loses exactly that entity).
    - For kinetics: shuffle the sample order (invariant).
  - (b) Every bed task is tagged witness-bearing (hash total, exclusion set, conservation, count) or witness-free. Every
    detector's recall is reported per tag.
- **Isomorph and source.** Equivalence modulo inputs (EMI): one compiler checked against its own variants, which are
  equivalent on the profiled inputs, found "147 confirmed, unique bug reports for GCC and LLVM" with no reference
  compiler [sweep B 2; P, https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~su/publications/emi.pdf]. Locally, the only detectors that fired
  are exact structural checks (P4).
- **Build.**
  - A `variants` hook in `experiments/jit/run-compiled.mjs`.
  - Per-family relation lists beside the oracles.
  - A `witness` field in the bed task metadata.
  - About 2 days.
- **Experiment and rule.**
  - Run (i) on the archived compiled versions over dev 201–224, where the wrong outputs are known.
  - Then run (ii) on a fresh dev range cleared through SEED-LEDGER. Kinetics 249–272 is still unused per the ledger;
    records needs a new range.
  - Adopt as a contract if it flags ≥ 50% of wrong compiled outputs at ≤ 10% false alarms on correct outputs in both
    (i) and (ii).
  - Kill below 25%.
  - Report recall per witness tag.
- **Cost.** In-solver Haiku judges on the variant inputs, ≤ $10 cap.
- **Kill mode.** The dominant errors (under-merges by threshold, date-convention reads) are invariant under shuffle and
  duplication by construction [I]. If they are, this catches order-dependence and accidental state only, and the row
  says so.
- **What the critique removed.** The draft's non-merge certificate tested on archived solvers is gone, for four reasons:
  - P16 already carries `BAND`, a judge and `weak-merge`;
  - the archived solvers were not built from P16;
  - the external DOB+phone estimator failed its own kill rule (AUROC 0.663 < 0.7, `DEV-NOTES.md:325-327`);
  - s214's under-merge was a date misread, not a near-miss on similarity.

### M2. Priced verification, set by the slider (P6, P7; PROOF-LEDGER K2)

- **Blocker.** Verification is the cost lever (P6), the slider has nothing to trade (P7), and the shadow schedule has no
  stated bound.
- **Mechanism.** A pure function in `experiments/jit/verify-economics.mjs`, fed by committed `results.jsonl` and
  `events.jsonl`.
  - **Inputs:** the arm, a shadow tier (the arm's interpreter, the Haiku agent, a code witness where one exists), and a
    shadow rate.
  - **Outputs:**
    - steady-state $ per task, with and without adjudication;
    - the break-even rate against each agent;
    - the system-view miss bound: a Wilson upper bound on detectable-wrong among randomly shadowed tasks, using the
      `shadowFloor` propensity that has already landed.
  - **The slider.** The owner sets a point on (cost, system-view miss bound), and the stream takes the cheapest (tier,
    rate) that meets it.
- **Isomorph and source.**
  - Continuous sampling plans: Dodge CSP-1 sets a worst-case long-run outgoing defect rate (AOQL) from (i, f)
    [sweep B 5; P, Hryniewicz]. Recomputed [M]: i = 20, f = 0.1 gives an AOQL of 5.23% (worst at p = 9.75%); i = 60,
    f = 0.25 gives 0.99%.
  - Risk-limiting audits bound the hand-count at about 4.8/m decisions per batch, independent of batch size
    [sweep B 13; P, Stark].
- **Why CSP-1 is not the default.**
  - With i = 20–60 it means near-100% inspection inside a 24-task stream.
  - Measured disagreement rates of 25–100% (D25 §4) would keep it at 100%.
  - The AOQL assumes a perfect inspector, and shadows share the producer's errors (s212).
  - So CSP-1 is offered only for long production streams. Dev streams keep the random floor.
- **Build.** About 150 lines plus a unit test over the committed streams; 1 day.
- **Experiment and rule.**
  - $0 on records and kinetics dev, then on R0-K.
  - If no (tier, rate) with rate ≥ the random floor beats the Haiku agent's $ per task, the PREREG H1 cost claim
    against the Haiku agent is withdrawn for these families. It is restated against the Sonnet or Opus agent, or as a
    reliability claim at a declared W.
  - If a code-witness tier exists for a family, report the cost with it.
- **Cost.** $0.
- **Kill mode.** The system-view bound is too wide to be useful at 24 tasks (a Wilson upper bound with n ≈ 6 shadows is
  0.39 at 0/6) [M]. The slider is then honest only for long streams, and the row says so.

### M3. A reuse acceptance rule that a perfect gate can pass (D7; PROOF-LEDGER K1)

- **Blocker.** `LIBRARY-TESTS.md:161` serves reuse only if the 95% Wilson upper bound is ≤ 0.05 at ≥ 60 decisions.
  0/60 gives 0.0602 [M].
- **Mechanism.** One of three, chosen by simulation:
  - (a) the minimal fix: a one-sided 95% bound (0/60 gives 0.043 [M]), or "evaluated at n ≥ 73";
  - (b) a Wald SPRT with p0 = 0.01, p1 = 0.05, α = β = 0.05: +1.609 per wrong reuse, −0.0412 per clean one,
    boundaries ±2.944, so 72 clean decisions accept [M];
  - (c) a fixed single-sampling plan with n = 181, c = 4, which has a 3.6% false kill at p = 0.01 and a 4.9% miss at
    p = 0.05 [M].

  Drop the early kill "≥ 20, point > 0.10", or fold it into the SPRT.
- **Isomorph and source.**
  - Audit attribute sampling fixes the plan in advance. Poloniecki et al. applied seven tests to the same 371
    transplants: three alarmed and four did not, and a retrospective choice makes the false-positive rate undefinable
    [sweep B 1; P].
  - BLOCKER-SYNTHESIS row 38 already plans an SPRT admission.
- **Build.** Functions in `experiments/lib/stats.mjs` and a simulation test at true rates 0, 0.01, 0.03 and 0.05, with
  decisions clustered by parent (Kish n). 1–2 hours.
- **Rule.** Adopt the rule with false kill ≤ 5% at p ≤ 0.01 and miss ≤ 5% at p ≥ 0.05, at L4's scale (about 580
  decisions).
- **Cost.** $0.
- **Kill mode.** Clustering by parent shrinks the effective n below what any rule needs at L4's size. L4 then reports a
  CI and no decision.

### M5. The retroactive sweep with early cutoff, tested on R3′ (F2; PROOF-LEDGER K5)

- **Blocker.** The designed sweep propagates by consumption and relabels every admission (`LIBRARY.md:398-417`). The dev

- **Mechanism.**
  - Re-execute each caller with the corrected helper, and stop where its output does not change. That means equal
    `outDigestOf` for deterministic code, and the tolerant comparator where judges sit inside.
  - Count derivations, so that an output with another valid derivation survives.
  - Record key data elements per output (h@v, judge model ids, policy hash, input digest), so that scoping is one
    lookup.
- **Isomorph and source.**
  - Build systems with verifying traces support early cutoff [sweep B 4; P, Build Systems à la Carte].
  - FSMA 204 lot tracing requires a sortable trace within 24 hours [sweep B 4; practice].
- **Substrate (new).**
  - `beds/wbed/r3p/impact.mjs` already computes, per planted defect, the callers that inherit it (`affected`) and those
    that call the defective helper only where it is right (`unaffected`).
  - `callLogOf` gives a production call log with an exact `wrong` flag per output.
  - `scoreImpact` and `scoreReplay` score exact precision and recall.
- **Build.** `experiments/jit/sweep.mjs` with consumption and cutoff modes; 1–2 days.
- **Experiment and rule.**
  - On R3′ dev worlds, compare consumption with cutoff on suspect callers and relabelled outputs.
  - Adopt cutoff if it removes ≥ 50% of false suspects at 100% recall of affected callers and wrong outputs.
  - Also report the share of LIBRARY §7.5 relabels that would be false `wrong` labels.
- **Cost.** $0. R3′ executes JavaScript.
- **Kill mode.** If unaffected callers are < 10% of callers in R3′, the refinement has no bite there and needs R4 call
  trees.

### M6. Distinguishing-input adjudication: a diagnostic, not a gate (A4; PROOF-LEDGER K13)

- **Blocker.** Acceptance shows no predictive signal (n = 19), and adjudication is periodic and common-mode.
- **Mechanism.** For competing versions, search inputs the bed generator can regenerate for one where the versions
  disagree. Only that input goes to the adjudicator.
- **Isomorph and source.** Brahma: oracle-guided synthesis with distinguishing inputs runs 1.5–12× faster than random
  inputs (checked in the downloaded text). Sweep A reports 3–13 against 5–25 queries
  [sweep A 5; P, https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2010/EECS-2010-15.pdf].
- **Why demoted** (critique 15).
  - Deployment has no generator.
  - The generator shares an author with the oracle (O10).
  - The adjudicator on a distinguishing input is again interpreter-class.
  - New inputs mean paid runs that consume seeds.
  - "≥ 80% of families" with 2 families means both.
- **Experiment.**
  - After R0-K, on its version pairs only: adjudications needed per decision, and whether the survivor passes the
    oracle.
  - Report only; no rule changes from it at K = 1.
- **Cost.** $5–20.
- **Kill mode.** Candidates that agree on everything regenerable and are both wrong (s214). The search then finds
  nothing.
